I study **Computer Science** and **Modern Culture & Media** at Brown University. I’m interested in how tech, power, and sociolegal understandings of new media co-produce each other.

In the past, I’ve had the luck to RA at Brown’s [DISCO Lab](https://discolab.cs.brown.edu/), where I researched and helped engineer AI-supported systems helping no-code communities design custom moderation tools on federated social media. [^1] I’ve also visualized open-source lobbying data for the Watson School of International and Public Affairs. [^1.5]
[^1]: How might community-based moderation demand different algorithmic affordances than top-down, centralized moderation? How can we use small AI thoughtfully?

Right now, I’m spending many waking hours working on my senior thesis, which considers artist communities' refusal of AI scraping by:
 - [hci] Taxonomizing the methods of individual and collective action artists take across different creative domains as a function of their sociolegal conditions.
    
 - [crypto/ML] Hardening commitment-based non-membership proofs to adversarial augmentation – such that these proofs can be used more robustly to guarantee that sensitive data hasn’t been used in AI training datasets.[^2] 
 [^2]: This helps policymakers and the public audit tech companies’ compliance to data protection regulations more quickly and verifiably, and may mean that the onus of proving violation rests less laboriously on the public - but of course, sociolegal integration is the other half of the question.
