I study **Computer Science** and **Modern Culture & Media** at Brown University. I’m interested in how tech, power, and sociolegal understandings of new media co-produce each other. Work I've done in the past--and hope to do in the future--concerns the design and implementation of AI/cryptographic systems centering community needs.

Currently, I'm doing work on the following:
 - Cryptographic and legal perspectives to machine unlearning
 - sociotechnical systems for anonymous, verifiable harm reporting

In the past, I’ve had the luck to RA at Brown’s [DISCO Lab](https://discolab.cs.brown.edu/), where I researched and co-engineered AI-supported systems helping non-programmer communities design moderation tools on federated social media. [^1] I’ve also visualized social networks in open-source lobbying data for the Watson School of International and Public Affairs. [^1.5]
[^1]: How might community-based moderation demand different algorithmic affordances than top-down, centralized moderation? How can we use small AI thoughtfully?
